Product in one paragraph

Checkpoint lets a team of AI agents buy things for a person or a group without ever holding their keys. The agents read a shopping list, collect real offers, compare them and propose one basket. The basket is paid only after three independent locks agree. Guard's deterministic rules must pass and Guard signs the exact basket. The human approves that exact basket on-chain. The Vault contract checks caps, the vendor allowlist, freshness and settlement safety itself. A Sputnik DAO owns the rules. Every decision and payout leaves verifiable evidence.

How to read this document. "Must" is required for the MVP. "Should" is wanted if time allows. "Not in MVP" is deliberately excluded. Priorities: P0 means the core demo cannot be called complete without it, P1 means include once P0 is stable, P2 is stretch. IDs are stable: FR-xx (functional), NFR-xx, INV-x (invariants), R-xx (Guard rules), AT-xx (acceptance tests), D-xx (open decisions). This PRD specifies behaviour. It is not evidence that anything is built. The companion Checkpoint-Task-List.pdf assigns every item to a person and a day.

1.2 Goals

  • G1. An AI agent can carry out purchases for an owner without holding any key that can move the owner's funds beyond what the owner approved.
  • G2. Every payout is bound to one exact basket that a human approved and Guard signed. Any change to a vendor, amount, token, owner, policy or expiry invalidates it.
  • G3. Hard limits (caps, vendor allowlist, token allowlist) are enforced by the Vault on-chain, whatever Guard or the agents say.
  • G4. Funds can never be paid twice, credited twice or spent while reserved, including when vendor transfers fail.
  • G5. Price decisions are deterministic, explainable and reproducible from published evidence. The LLM explains them and never decides them.
  • G6. A DAO owns the rules, and a limited guardian can only pause.
  • G7. A stranger, or the AI screener, can clone the repo and reproduce the core loop from the README, with or without testnet keys.

1.3 Non-goals (not in the MVP)

  • Real money, mainnet writes, real merchant checkout, and delivery or fulfilment guarantees.
  • Live web scraping across many categories, or a general shopping assistant.
  • Settlement through NEAR Intents, cross-chain payment, Chain Signatures.
  • DEX swaps, yield, trading (P2 at most).
  • Commons, missions, bounties, reputation, policy templates (from the Expansion Proposal).
  • ML-based fraud or exploit detection, or any claim of protecting funds outside the Vault.

1.4 Release success criteria

  1. From a clean checkout, pnpm demo:sandbox runs the full loop (list → offers → verdicts → basket → human approval → Guard signature → payouts → audit) on a local NEAR sandbox. It needs no testnet account and no LLM key.
  2. The same loop runs on testnet through the web app with a real wallet, and every step links to a transaction.
  3. All P0 acceptance tests in §16 pass and are recorded with command output or transaction hashes.
  4. One real Sputnik DAO vote changes a Vault limit on testnet, and a non-DAO attempt fails.
  5. The README states what is on-chain, off-chain, a stand-in and not claimed. The repo is public, the X post is live, and the form is submitted by 14 Nov 12:00 UTC.

2. Problem and evidence

Agents now hold wallets. Autonomous Business, Agent Economy and AI Swarms all describe agents that "spend, negotiate and pay". The simplest way to let an agent pay is to give it a full-access key or a hot wallet. A prompt injection, a bug or a stolen key then drains everything. The AI Security & Defense track names the need directly: "Give AI money without giving it the keys to everything."

EvidenceWhat happenedLesson Checkpoint applies
NEAR Intents, Sep–Oct 2026 (about $3.87M; BlockSec, CertiK)A refund and callback path left internal credit spendable while the backing assets had been returned. It went unnoticed for about 40 hours.Reserve before external calls. Settle only from the callback result, exactly once. Restore only what provably never left. Reconcile ledger against real balance and pause on deficit (INV-1–INV-4, FR-S).
Rhea Finance, Apr 2026 (about $18.4M; QuillAudits)A check counted an intermediate amount as final value.The Vault verifies the exact amounts it pays and never trusts a client-computed total (Σ payouts = total, FR-A08).
LLM agents in generalLLMs can be steered by untrusted text, such as a product listing that says "ignore your rules".The LLM never decides eligibility, amounts or recipients. Schemas, deterministic rules and the Vault do (§13).

We do not claim Checkpoint would have prevented those incidents inside those protocols. We apply their design lessons to our own Vault.

7. End-to-end flows, including failures

7.1 Setup (scripted: pnpm setup:testnet)

  1. Create accounts (faucet-sponsored via near-cli-rs), deploy cpUSD, register Vault, owners and vendors 01–05 on cpUSD.
  2. Deploy the Vault with new(config): token allowlist [cpUSD], vendors 01–04 and 06 allowed, caps, max vendors = 4, TTL ≤ 30 min, rules hash, Guard public key v1, guardian, and dao_id = the setup account for now.
  3. Create the Sputnik DAO with the three council members. Set Vault dao_id to the DAO (a one-time transfer_admin by the setup account). From then on, only the DAO can change policy.
  4. Mint cpUSD to owner accounts. Add the Settlement Agent's function-call key.

7.2 Deposit and agent authorisation

  1. The owner calls cpusd.ft_transfer_call(receiver_id=vault, amount, msg="") from the wallet (1 yoctoNEAR).
  2. The token calls vault.ft_on_transfer(sender_id, amount, msg). The Vault checks that the predecessor is an allowlisted token and that no deficit is flagged. It credits available[owner][token], increases liabilities[token], emits deposit and returns "0". Otherwise it returns the full amount, which the token refunds.
  3. The owner calls set_agent(agent.cpdemo.testnet, true) (1 yocto) to authorise the Settlement Agent for their funds.

7.3 Purchase request: the happy path

  1. Request. The owner enters a list, e.g. "2× VPS 2 vCPU/4 GB for 1 month, 1× .com domain for 1 year, 1 TB object storage for 1 month", plus optional constraints (budget ≤ policy cap, preferred vendors). The web app posts to POST /v1/requests.
  2. Parse. The Research Agent turns the text into Item[] with the LLM (structured output, validated by Zod) or the deterministic parser. Each item must map to a catalogue item_key in the snapshot. Unknown items are reported back, never guessed.
  3. Research. For each item the agent loads every snapshot offer with that item_key and streams research.offer events.
  4. Assess. The agents call Guard POST /v1/assess with offer IDs only. Guard loads the offers itself, computes medians and runs R-01–R-10, returning a verdict and findings for each offer.
  5. Basket. The Coordinator picks, for each item, the cheapest offer with verdict ALLOW whose vendor is allowlisted. If none exists, it picks the cheapest REVIEW offer and marks it needs-acknowledgement. It groups lines by vendor and enforces ≤ 4 vendors (it re-plans by moving lines to the next-cheapest allowed vendor). Then it computes totals and emits coordinator.basket.
  6. Acknowledge. If any line is REVIEW, the UI shows why, and the owner ticks "I reviewed this" (POST /v1/requests/:id/acknowledge).
  7. Sign. The agents call Guard POST /v1/sign-basket. Guard re-runs every offer rule and basket rule R-20–R-24 against live Vault views (policy, remaining daily allowance, available balance). Then it builds the Borsh payload, computes the hash and signs it. It returns {payload_b64, hash_hex, signature_b64, expires_at_ms}.
  8. Human approval. The UI decodes the payload and shows every payout line, vendor, amount, total, expiry and hash. The owner sends vault.approve_basket(hash, expires_at_ms) with 1 yocto, which forces a full-access-key signature in the wallet.
  9. Execute. The Settlement Agent polls get_approval(hash). Once the approval exists, it calls execute_basket(payload_b64, signature_b64) with 300 TGas using its function-call key.
  10. Settle. The Vault runs every check in FR-A, marks the hash used, deletes the approval, reserves the total and adds it to today's spend. It creates one payout operation per vendor and calls cpusd.ft_transfer(vendor, amount, memo=basket_id), each followed by private on_payout(basket_hash, index).
  11. Callback. On success the reserved amount drops and the payout is Completed. On failure, the reserved amount moves back to available, the daily spend is reduced and the payout is Failed. When all payouts are final, the basket becomes Completed, PartiallyPaid or Failed.
  12. Audit. The agents service stores the evidence bundle (request, offers, verdicts, basket, payload, hashes, transaction hashes). Its evidence_hash is inside the signed payload and emitted in basket_executed, so anyone can verify the bundle served at /v1/audit/:basket_id.

7.5 Withdrawal

The owner calls withdraw(token, amount) with 1 yocto. The Vault requires available ≥ amount and no deficit. It moves the amount from available to withdrawing, calls ft_transfer(owner, amount) and then on_withdraw. On success it reduces liabilities. On failure it restores available. Withdrawals are allowed while paused.

7.6 DAO policy change

  1. A council member creates a Sputnik FunctionCall proposal: receiver vault.cpdemo.testnet, method set_limits, args base64 JSON, deposit "1" yocto, gas 50 TGas. The proposal bond (read with get_policy, default 1 NEAR) is attached.
  2. Council members vote act_proposal(id, "VoteApprove"). The default policy needs ½ of the council, so 2 of 3.
  3. On approval, the DAO calls the Vault. The Vault checks predecessor == dao_id and 1 yocto, validates bounds, applies the change, increments policy_version and emits policy_updated.
  4. Approvals signed under the old version now fail (AT-07). The web app shows the new limits and the proposal history.

9. Guard rules engine specification

9.1 Definitions

  • Valid offer: has seller, vendor_id mapping, source_url, captured_at, currency USD, a total landed price including fees, and a quantity > 0, and passes the spec check for its item_key.
  • Normalised unit price p: total landed price ÷ quantity in item units, in integer micro-USD and floored.
  • Median m: median of p over valid, de-duplicated offers for the same item_key, including the offer being assessed. With an even count, it is the floored mean of the two middle values.
  • Ratio in basis points: r = floor(p × 10 000 / m). There is no floating-point anywhere in the rules.
  • Verdict for an offer = the most severe level among its findings (BLOCK > REVIEW > ALLOW). All findings are reported.

Borsh is serialised by borsh (Rust derive) and by the borsh npm package with an explicit schema in packages/shared/src/payload.ts. Strings are u32-length-prefixed UTF-8. If the Rust and TS hashes ever disagree, the vector decides and the side that disagrees is the bug.

12. Security model, invariants and threats

12.1 Invariants (each asserted in tests after every step)

IDInvariant
INV-1At every stable point, for every token T: ft_balance_of(vault, T) ≥ liabilities[T], and liabilities[T] = Σ owners (available + reserved + withdrawing).
INV-2Reserved funds are never spendable or withdrawable. They leave reserved only through exactly one callback per payout.
INV-3A basket hash executes at most once (used_hashes), and an approval is consumed by execution.
INV-4No payout reaches a vendor outside the allowlist, and no payout exceeds what the approved and signed payload names.
INV-5No state change happens in execute_basket unless every check passes (checks first, effects next, interactions last).
INV-6Today's spend for an owner never exceeds the effective daily cap at the moment of reservation.
INV-7Policy can change only through dao_id (and the guardian's pause), and every change increments policy_version.

22.2 Glossary

Basket: the set of payouts for one request. Payload: the Borsh bytes describing a basket. Hash: sha256 of domain + payload, the thing that is approved and signed. Function-call key: a limited NEAR key that can call named methods on one contract and cannot attach deposits. 1 yocto: the smallest NEAR unit; requiring it forces a full-access key. Callback: the follow-up function that handles an async call's result. Stand-in: a team-controlled testnet account representing a seller. bps: basis points (1/100 of a percent). Evidence bundle: everything the agents saw and decided, hashed into the payload.
