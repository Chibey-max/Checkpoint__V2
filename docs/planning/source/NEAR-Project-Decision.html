The decision

Merge the two projects into one submission. Build Checkpoint's Guard and Vault as the foundation. Use the Agent Procurement Desk's agents as the AI that Checkpoint keeps under control: the agents find and compare offers, and nothing gets paid unless the rules, a human and the Vault all agree.

Enter AI Security & Defense as the primary track, with Autonomous Business and Agent Economy as secondary tracks. Do not submit two projects.

Why, in four points

  1. The Agent Procurement Desk breaks the official rules as written. Its "never cut" milestone M4 says the basket "settles on testnet via Intents". HACKATHON.md Q-007 says an Intents testnet does not exist and is not planned. Fixing that requires a contract that holds funds, caps spending and checks an exact approval. Checkpoint's Vault already is that contract.
  2. Checkpoint on its own is valid, but it is weakest exactly where the Procurement Desk is strong. Its own review asks "What does AI mean in this MVP?" (Finding 12, D-09). Its demo also depends on a testnet DEX that nobody has confirmed will deploy (D-01). The Procurement Desk brings a real AI agent that spends real (test) money, and a demo a judge can follow in one minute.
  3. The merged product is the Security track's own description. The track text reads: "Give AI money without giving it the keys to everything." Autonomous Business names "procurement" explicitly. Agent Economy covers agents that "spend… and pay".
  4. "Submit both" is not really an option. Q-015 allows two projects only if they are independent and each README names the other. If the Procurement Desk reused Checkpoint's Vault, the two would not be independent. Building two independent stacks with three people in nine days means two half-finished entries. Both screening reports warn against exactly that.
The merger also fails safely. If the agent layer runs late, what is left is still Checkpoint, with a scripted agent in place of the swarm. That is a complete AI Security & Defense submission, so the merger costs no fallback position.

3. Why the merger works

The two designs already line up part for part. The Procurement Desk's Policy Agent returns AUTO_APPROVE, MANUAL_REVIEW or BLOCK. Checkpoint's Guard returns allow, warn or block. The Assessment asks for an approval "cryptographically bound to the exact basket, recipients, amounts and expiry". That is PRD requirement FR-014, almost word for word.

4. The merged product

Name: keep Checkpoint, and call the demo app Procurement Desk. Suggested one-liner: "Checkpoint lets a team of AI agents buy things for you without ever holding your keys."

How a purchase moves through it

  1. Rules. The DAO sets the vendor allowlist, the per-basket and daily caps and the price thresholds. The owner deposits test tokens into the Vault.
  2. Research. The Research Agent turns a shopping list into items and collects at least 3 offers per item from a captured set of real listings. It normalises unit prices and keeps each offer's source, seller and timestamp.
  3. Guard rules. Each offer gets allow, warn or block from deterministic rules: ≤105% of the median is allowed, 105–120% needs review, >120% is blocked, <50% needs review, fewer than 3 offers needs review, and a vendor not on the allowlist or an over-cap total is blocked. The LLM explains the outcome. It does not decide it.
  4. Basket. The Coordinator builds one basket from allowed offers and any reviewed offers the user ticks. Blocked offers cannot enter. The basket is hashed in a canonical encoding.
  5. Human approval. The user reviews the basket and sends one wallet transaction: approve_basket(hash, expiry).
  6. Guard signature. Guard signs the same basket with the Vault ID, network, nonce, expiry and policy version.
  7. Settlement. The Settlement Agent can only call the Vault. It submits the basket and the signature. The Vault checks that a live human approval exists for that hash, the Guard signature, nonce, expiry, policy version, the vendor allowlist, the caps and the available balance. It then reserves the funds and makes one payout per vendor.
  8. Audit. A private callback settles each payout exactly once. A failed payout is restored only after the funds are verified, and otherwise goes to "Needs Reconciliation". Every step emits an event that the UI links to its transaction.
Lock 1: GuardThe price and vendor rules must pass, and Guard signs only an exact, fresh basket.
Lock 2: HumanOne on-chain approval of one exact basket. Any change voids it.
Lock 3: VaultCaps, allowlist, nonce, reservation and callback-safe settlement, enforced on-chain.

The agents hold none of the three locks. A stolen agent key can call one Vault method, and that method checks all three.

8. Decisions to make today

  1. Agree to the merger and a single submission.
  2. Name: keep "Checkpoint", with "Procurement Desk" as the demo app.
  3. Pick one product category with standard units and at least 3 comparable sellers per item.
  4. Test token: I recommend deploying our own NEP-141 token, so we control minting and storage registration and can stage B-09 on purpose.
  5. Freeze the basket payload: field list, canonical encoding and a domain-separation string, with a test vector shared by the contract and Guard owners.
  6. Put names on the three workstreams.
  7. Confirm which day-1 checks from the Appendix are already done.
